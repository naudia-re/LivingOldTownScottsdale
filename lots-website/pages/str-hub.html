The Rules

Scottsdale STR rules, in plain english

The headlines you should know before you start touring. Not legal advice — and the regulations evolve. Always confirm with your agent and the City of Scottsdale before you offer.

01

You Need a Permit

Every STR operating in Scottsdale must register with the City and carry an active permit. There's an annual fee, an inspection requirement, and a permit number that must appear on every listing.

02

Liability Insurance Is Required

Scottsdale mandates a minimum liability policy specifically covering short-term rental use. Generic homeowner policies don't qualify — you need an STR endorsement or a dedicated STR policy.

03

HOAs Can Still Say No

A City permit doesn't override your HOA. Most Old Town condos are NOT STR-eligible — only a specific subset of buildings have CC&Rs that allow rentals under 30 days. We track the approved list for you.

04

Noise & Occupancy Caps Are Enforced

Scottsdale has real teeth here: documented complaints, occupancy limits tied to bedroom count, and quiet hours that are actively monitored. One bad guest can cost you a permit suspension.

Why the right Scottsdale STR agent matters more than the building

Short-term rentals are their own discipline inside Scottsdale real estate. The local rules, permitting, HOA and CC&R risk, insurance, and an honest read on which properties are actually worth it — the agent you pick makes all the difference.

Most agents in Phoenix can show you an Old Town Scottsdale home. Very few actually specialize in short-term rentals out here. The difference between the two is everything.

No one can promise you how a property will perform — not us, not anyone. What an experienced local STR agent can do is tell you the truth: which locations and homes make sense, what the City permit and tax steps really involve, and which properties are “DUDD” — the ones that look good on paper but don't sell because they never actually profit. Which lenders actually write loans on STR-friendly properties (most won't). Which insurance carriers carry a real Scottsdale STR endorsement. Which property managers, cleaners, photographers, and supply vendors are worth bringing in — and which to avoid.

FAQ

Old Town Scottsdale STR investing, answered

The questions we get every week from first-time STR buyers, out-of-state investors, and portfolio scalers. The honest answers, not the brochure version.

Can you legally Airbnb a property in Old Town Scottsdale?

Yes — with the right setup. Short-term rentals are legal in Scottsdale, but every operator must register with the City, carry an active permit, list the permit number on every listing, and hold STR-specific liability insurance. Beyond the City layer, your HOA's CC&Rs control whether your specific building allows rentals under 30 days. Most Old Town condos do not allow it — only a specific subset (Optima Kierland, The Mark, Vue Scottsdale, 7180 Optima, Envy, and a few others) have STR-friendly CC&Rs. Out here, most short-term rentals are single-family homes for exactly this reason. We confirm CC&Rs and pull HOA meeting minutes for any property we help you evaluate.

Which Old Town Scottsdale condo buildings allow short-term rentals?

The current short list of STR-friendly Old Town condo communities includes Optima Kierland, The Mark, Vue Scottsdale, 7180 Optima, Envy, and Inspiration Pointe. A handful of other buildings allow 30+ day rentals only, which is a different financial model. The list shifts — HOAs occasionally amend CC&Rs to restrict or unlock STR use. We keep the live list updated and flag buildings at risk of policy change. See the current list above (account required).

How do short-term rentals perform in Old Town Scottsdale?

Here's the honest answer: no one can promise how any specific property will perform, and as a licensed agent we don't project returns or build income forecasts. Performance depends on the home, the operator, pricing, seasonality, and plenty of factors outside anyone's control. Be skeptical of anyone quoting you a flat "Old Town STRs return X%." What we can do is give you the local rules, point you to the locations and homes that make sense, and steer you away from the “DUDD” properties — the ones that look good on paper but don't sell because they don't profit. We simply won't show you those.

Do I need a Scottsdale STR permit before closing?

No — the City of Scottsdale STR permit is filed after you take title, since the permit attaches to the property and owner. But you should not close without confirming that the building allows STR use and the unit can be permitted. We pull HOA documents and confirm Scottsdale zoning compatibility before you waive contingencies. Once you close, the permit application takes ~30 days, requires an inspection, and renews annually.

I'm buying from out of state — can I do this remotely?

Constantly. Most of our STR buyers are out of state. We handle FaceTime walkthroughs, detailed video tours, drone footage for the lot, and condition-specific recordings before you board a plane. We coordinate inspections, sign-and-deliver disclosures via DocuSign, and connect you with vetted property managers so the unit is operating before you even see it in person. We've closed Scottsdale STRs for buyers in California, Washington, New York, Texas, and Canada — the playbook is the playbook.

Can my existing real estate agent help me with an STR, or do I need a specialist?

Honest answer: most agents can help you buy a Scottsdale property, but only an STR specialist can help you buy one that operates. The HOA and CC&R risk read, the permit and tax steps, the lender connections, the insurance carriers, the post-close vendor network, and an honest take on which properties are worth it — that's a specialty discipline. Buying an STR with a generic agent isn't dangerous in the transaction, but it's easy to end up with a property that never should have been an STR in the first place.

What's the difference between investing in an Old Town condo vs. a single-family home for STR?

Out here, most short-term rentals are single-family homes rather than condos — because the majority of Old Town condo buildings don't allow rentals under 30 days. The homes that tend to make the most sense sit outside an HOA in 85250, 85251, or 85257 with a pool and 3+ bedrooms, and can accommodate group bookings, though they require more active management and carry higher operating costs (pool service, landscaping, larger turnovers). The few STR-friendly condo communities are simpler operationally but more limited on guest count. The right answer depends on your goals, capital, and how hands-on you want to be — and we'll walk you through the trade-offs honestly.
